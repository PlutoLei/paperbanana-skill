# Conceptual Art

Idea-driven compositions where the concept takes precedence over traditional aesthetics

## Design Aesthetic

Intellectually provocative style where visual execution serves the underlying idea. Inspired by Sol LeWitt, Joseph Kosuth, and Barbara Kruger, the aesthetic prioritizes meaning over beauty. Text, diagrams, and stark visual contrasts are used as primary artistic elements. Deliberately anti-decorative, with a gallery-installation feel.

## Background

- Color: Gallery White (#FAFAFA) or Stark Off-White (#F0F0F0)
- Texture: Clean matte surface, deliberately bare and institutional

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Bold condensed sans-serif or slab-serif with commanding presence. Text functions as visual art, not just information. May use Futura Bold, Helvetica Neue Black, or stark industrial faces.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Neutral sans-serif in regular weight. Clinical and objective. Sometimes monospaced for a documentary or archival feeling.

## Visual Elements

- Large-scale typographic statements as visual centerpieces
- Stark black-and-white contrasts
- Numbered lists or instruction-style layouts
- Framed text blocks presented as art objects
- Deliberately empty space as a compositional choice
- Documentary-style photography (monochrome)
- Diagrammatic arrows and flowcharts as expression
- Found-object collage elements

## Style Rules

### Do

- Let the idea drive every visual decision
- Use text as the primary visual element
- Embrace stark, high-contrast compositions
- Leave deliberate voids and negative space
- Question conventional presentation aesthetics

### Don't

- Add purely decorative elements
- Use soft colors or gentle gradients
- Include ornamental borders or frames
- Apply illustrative or painterly techniques
- Add slide numbers, footers, or logos

## Best For

Contemporary art discussions, philosophy seminars, social commentary presentations, curatorial proposals, academic critiques, thought leadership talks, manifesto-style pitches
