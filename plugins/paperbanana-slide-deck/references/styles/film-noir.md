# Film Noir

1940s cinematic style with high-contrast monochrome, dramatic shadows, and mysterious atmosphere

## Design Aesthetic

Moody and suspenseful compositions channeling the visual language of 1940s detective cinema. Stark contrasts between deep shadows and harsh highlights create a world of moral ambiguity and visual drama. Venetian blind shadows, rain-slicked streets, and cigarette smoke define the atmospheric vocabulary.

## Background

- Color: Noir Black (#0D0D0D)
- Texture: Film grain overlay with subtle vignette darkening at edges

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Bodoni Moda or similar high-contrast serif. Dramatic thick-thin strokes mirror the chiaroscuro of noir cinematography. Elegant yet dangerous.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Inconsolata or similar monospace for body text. Typewriter quality evoking detective case files and newspaper columns.

## Visual Elements

- Venetian blind shadow patterns across surfaces
- Dramatic diagonal light shafts in darkness
- Film grain texture overlay on all imagery
- Silhouette figures in doorways and alleys
- Rain streaks and wet reflective surfaces
- Cigarette smoke wisps and fog effects
- Harsh single-source lighting with deep shadows
- Vignette framing drawing focus to center

## Style Rules

### Do

- Use extreme contrast between light and shadow
- Create mystery through selective lighting
- Apply film grain for authentic cinematic texture
- Keep compositions dramatically lit from a single source
- Use diagonal compositions for visual tension

### Don't

- Include bright, colorful, or vibrant elements
- Use flat, evenly lit compositions
- Add cheerful or optimistic imagery
- Include modern or contemporary visual elements
- Create blurry or low-contrast scenes

## Best For

Mystery and thriller presentations, film studies talks, detective story showcases, photography exhibitions, dramatic narrative presentations, vintage entertainment branding
