# De Stijl

Neoplastic grid compositions with primary colors, black lines, and utopian harmony

## Design Aesthetic

Rooted in the De Stijl movement of Mondrian and Van Doesburg. Strict rectilinear grids divide the canvas into blocks of primary color and white. Heavy black lines form the structural skeleton. The style seeks universal harmony through radical reduction to horizontal and vertical elements, primary hues, and balanced asymmetry.

## Background

- Color: Pure White (#FFFFFF)
- Texture: Flat, textureless surface; emphasis on pure color fields

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Geometric sans-serif in bold weight, strictly horizontal or vertical alignment. Clean, constructed letterforms that feel like part of the grid. Often all-caps.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Light-weight geometric sans-serif. Minimal, functional, subordinate to the grid composition. Always aligned to horizontal axes.

## Visual Elements

- Heavy black horizontal and vertical lines forming an asymmetric grid
- Rectangular color blocks of red, blue, and yellow at varying scales
- Large white negative space as an active compositional element
- Content placed within grid cells, never overlapping boundaries
- Typography treated as part of the grid structure
- No diagonals, curves, or organic shapes whatsoever
- Scale variation between color blocks creates visual rhythm
- Minimal iconography reduced to rectilinear forms

## Style Rules

### Do

- Use only primary colors (red, blue, yellow) plus black and white
- Build all compositions on horizontal and vertical lines exclusively
- Maintain asymmetric balance across the grid
- Treat negative space as equally important to filled space
- Keep all elements strictly rectilinear

### Don't

- Introduce diagonal lines, curves, or circles
- Use secondary or tertiary colors
- Add textures, gradients, or shadows
- Create complex patterns or decorative elements
- Add slide numbers, footers, or logos

## Best For

Design theory education, art history presentations, architecture and interior design, modern art discussions, minimalist brand identity, abstract composition studies
