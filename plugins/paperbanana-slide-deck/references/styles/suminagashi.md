# Suminagashi

Japanese floating ink marbling with delicate, ethereal patterns and meditative elegance

## Design Aesthetic

The oldest form of marbling, suminagashi ("floating ink") creates hypnotic concentric patterns by gently dropping ink onto water and letting surface tension guide its flow. The resulting prints are meditative and unpredictable -- delicate rings, feathered edges, and subtle ink concentrations form patterns of extraordinary refinement. The aesthetic embodies Japanese wabi-sabi: beauty in imperfection, simplicity, and the natural behavior of materials.

## Background

- Color: Washi White (#FAF8F3)
- Texture: Japanese washi paper with visible kozo fibers

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Noto Serif JP or similar refined serif with Japanese character support. Light to regular weight. Restrained elegance with cultural resonance.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Noto Sans or similar clean sans-serif. Light weight with generous spacing. Quiet and contemplative.

## Visual Elements

- Concentric ring patterns from ink drops on water
- Delicate feathered edges where ink meets water boundary
- Subtle ink concentration variations
- Organic flowing curves guided by water movement
- Near-monochromatic tonal compositions
- Visible washi paper fibers beneath ink
- Comb-dragged directional patterns
- Gentle gradation from dense to dilute ink

## Style Rules

### Do

- Keep the palette restrained and near-monochromatic
- Let patterns form organically without forced structure
- Show the paper texture as an integral element
- Maintain delicacy and refinement in all patterns
- Embrace asymmetry and natural imperfection

### Don't

- Use bright saturated multi-color palettes
- Create rigid geometric patterns
- Add heavy bold graphic elements
- Include western decorative motifs
- Make patterns too dense or busy
