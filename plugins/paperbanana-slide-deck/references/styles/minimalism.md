# Minimalism

Pure reductive design with essential geometric forms, hard edges, and maximum visual restraint

## Design Aesthetic

Radical simplicity that strips every composition down to its absolute essentials. Inspired by Donald Judd, Agnes Martin, and Dieter Rams, the style uses clean geometric shapes, hard edges, and vast empty space to create focused, meditative compositions. Every element that remains has earned its place. The absence of decoration becomes the strongest statement.

## Background

- Color: Pure White (#FFFFFF) or Near-White (#FAFAFA)
- Texture: None -- perfectly flat, immaculate surface

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Thin geometric sans-serif with perfect proportions. Helvetica Neue Light, Inter, or similar rational faces. Extreme letter-spacing for an airy, spacious feel.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Regular-weight geometric sans-serif from the same family. Clean, neutral, and invisible in the best sense. Functional without personality.

## Visual Elements

- Vast empty space as the dominant design element
- Single geometric shapes (circle, square, line) as focal points
- Hairline rules and delicate dividers
- Monochromatic or single-accent-color schemes
- Hard-edged shapes with no blur or shadow
- Grid-aligned placement with mathematical precision
- Sleek contours and clean silhouettes
- Absolute symmetry or deliberate asymmetric balance

## Style Rules

### Do

- Remove every element that is not absolutely necessary
- Use empty space as the primary communicative tool
- Limit to one accent color maximum
- Maintain razor-sharp edges and precise alignment
- Let content breathe with generous margins

### Don't

- Add decorative elements of any kind
- Use multiple colors, patterns, or textures
- Include illustrations or photographs
- Create dense or information-heavy layouts
- Add slide numbers, footers, or logos

## Best For

Architecture firm presentations, high-end product reveals, design philosophy talks, executive briefings, meditation and focus content, premium brand guidelines, gallery exhibition proposals
