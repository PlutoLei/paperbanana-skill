# Shepard Fairey Style

Street art activism with iconic stencil portraits, bold typography, and high-contrast red-black-white palette

## Design Aesthetic

Influenced by Shepard Fairey's OBEY and Hope poster aesthetics. High-contrast stencil-style portraiture meets political street art. Bold propaganda-inspired compositions use a restricted palette of red, black, cream, and blue. Typography is direct and impactful. Every element communicates urgency, authority, and cultural commentary.

## Background

- Color: Cream (#F5F0E1) or Deep Red (#8B1A1A)
- Texture: Wheat-paste poster texture with torn edges and urban grit

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Heavy condensed sans-serif or stencil face. All-caps with tight tracking. Industrial, commanding presence. Often arranged in banner or ribbon formations.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Medium-weight condensed sans-serif. Clean and functional, subordinate to the imagery. Set in structured blocks.

## Visual Elements

- High-contrast stencil-style portrait illustrations
- Radiating line patterns behind central figures
- Star and gear motifs as decorative graphic elements
- Banner ribbons carrying slogans and key messages
- Halftone dot patterns for mid-tone rendering
- Bold geometric framing with thick borders
- Iconic symbol repetition for visual rhythm
- Wheat-paste texture and torn-edge overlays

## Style Rules

### Do

- Maintain strict red, black, cream, and blue palette
- Use high-contrast stencil rendering for all imagery
- Create bold, iconic compositions with central focal points
- Apply street art texture and urban grit overlays
- Keep typography heavy, direct, and commanding

### Don't

- Use muted, pastel, or traditional color palettes
- Include photographic or soft-rendered imagery
- Create subtle, understated compositions
- Use delicate or decorative typefaces
- Add slide numbers, footers, or logos

## Best For

Political commentary, social activism presentations, street art and culture talks, brand rebellion narratives, music and counterculture content, campaign materials, protest art education
