# Op Art

Bold geometric optical illusions creating mesmerizing impressions of movement and depth

## Design Aesthetic

Rooted in the tradition of Bridget Riley and Victor Vasarely, this style exploits geometric patterns to produce powerful optical illusions. High-contrast black-and-white or carefully calibrated color combinations create sensations of vibrating movement, warping, and three-dimensional depth on a flat surface.

## Background

- Color: Pure White (#FFFFFF) or Pure Black (#000000)
- Texture: None -- pristine flat surface to maximize optical effect

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Futura Bold or similar geometric sans-serif. Clean, precise letterforms that integrate seamlessly with geometric patterns. All caps for maximum impact.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Helvetica Neue Light or similar neutral sans-serif. Minimal visual interference with the optical patterns. Highly legible.

## Visual Elements

- Concentric circles creating tunnel or vortex effects
- Parallel wavy lines producing moiré patterns
- Checkerboard distortions suggesting curvature
- Stripe patterns with graduated spacing
- Geometric tessellations with shifting perspectives
- Black-and-white contrast maximized for visual vibration
- Precise mathematical spacing and repetition
- Radial patterns emanating from center points

## Style Rules

### Do

- Maintain mathematical precision in all patterns
- Use maximum contrast for strongest optical effect
- Keep compositions centered or symmetrically balanced
- Limit color palette to 2-3 colors per composition
- Allow the pattern itself to be the primary content

### Don't

- Add organic or hand-drawn elements
- Use soft gradients or muted tones
- Include photographic imagery
- Break the geometric regularity with random elements
- Overcomplicate with too many competing patterns

## Best For

Design conferences, perception and psychology talks, mathematics presentations, art exhibitions, branding showcases, visual science lectures
