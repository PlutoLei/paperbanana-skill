# Monochrome

High-contrast black and white style emphasizing tone, texture, and dramatic visual depth

## Design Aesthetic

Bold compositions built entirely from black, white, and the full spectrum of grays. Contrast and tonal range replace color to create visual interest and depth. Rich textures and careful gradation give every element weight and presence, evoking the timeless power of black-and-white photography and printmaking.

## Background

- Color: Off White (#F5F5F5)
- Texture: Subtle paper grain or fine halftone dot pattern

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Bebas Neue or similar high-contrast condensed sans-serif. Bold, impactful letterforms with strong vertical emphasis. Commands attention through sheer tonal weight.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Source Serif Pro or similar classic serif for body text. Clear contrast between thick and thin strokes mirrors the monochrome aesthetic.

## Color Palette

| Role | Color | Hex | Usage |
|------|-------|-----|-------|
| Background | Off White | #F5F5F5 | Primary background |
| Primary Text | True Black | #000000 | Headlines, key text |
| Body Text | Dark Gray | #333333 | Body copy, descriptions |
| Mid Tone | Medium Gray | #808080 | Supporting elements, borders |
| Light Fill | Silver | #C0C0C0 | Card backgrounds, fills |
| Highlight | Bright White | #FFFFFF | Inset panels, contrast elements |
| Shadow | Deep Charcoal | #1A1A1A | Drop shadows, depth layers |

## Visual Elements

- High-contrast tonal photography or illustration
- Halftone and crosshatch textures for shading
- Bold geometric shapes in black and white
- Dramatic shadow play and silhouettes
- Grayscale gradient overlays for depth
- Thick and thin line weight contrast
- Duotone treatments on any imagery
- Strong figure-ground relationships

## Style Rules

### Do

- Maintain rich tonal range from pure black to pure white
- Use texture and contrast to create visual interest
- Create depth through layered gray values
- Keep compositions bold and high-impact
- Use scale contrast between large and small elements

### Don't

- Introduce any color, even desaturated tints
- Use noisy, blurry, or low-contrast compositions
- Create flat layouts without tonal variation
- Include vibrant or colorful imagery
- Use decorative elements that lack tonal purpose

## Best For

Photography portfolios, editorial design, art exhibitions, brand identity presentations, print design showcases, dramatic storytelling
