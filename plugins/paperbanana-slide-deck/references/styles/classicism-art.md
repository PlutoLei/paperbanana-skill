# Classicism Art

Harmonious elegance inspired by Greco-Roman ideals of clarity, balance, and noble simplicity

## Design Aesthetic

Refined and balanced visual language drawing from Greek and Roman classical traditions. Symmetrical compositions, restrained color palettes, and idealized proportions convey timeless authority. Architectural motifs and classical references create an atmosphere of intellectual gravitas and enduring beauty.

## Background

- Color: Marble White (#F2EFEA) or Parchment Cream (#F5F0E1)
- Texture: Polished marble surface with subtle veining, or fine linen canvas

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Elegant transitional serif inspired by Baskerville or Didot. High contrast between thick and thin strokes. Refined capitals with classical proportions, often with small caps for subtitles.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Well-proportioned old-style serif with moderate contrast. Warm and dignified with excellent readability. Book-quality typesetting with generous line spacing.

## Visual Elements

- Ionic and Corinthian column frames
- Laurel wreath borders and dividers
- Classical key pattern (meander/Greek key) borders
- Symmetrical compositions with central focal points
- Sculptural bust or figure silhouettes as accents
- Pediment-shaped title blocks
- Balanced negative space conveying restraint
- Engraved line-art illustrations

## Style Rules

### Do

- Maintain strict symmetry in layouts
- Use classical proportions (golden ratio) for spacing
- Keep ornamentation refined and purposeful
- Employ muted, earthy tones
- Reference architectural and sculptural forms

### Don't

- Use asymmetric or chaotic compositions
- Apply bright saturated or neon colors
- Include modern graphic elements or patterns
- Use casual or playful typography
- Add slide numbers, footers, or logos

## Best For

Philosophy and humanities lectures, museum exhibit guides, classical literature presentations, law and governance talks, university ceremonies, heritage brand storytelling, historical architecture reviews
