# Ukiyo-e

Traditional Japanese woodblock print style with vibrant colors, intricate detail, and cultural depth

## Design Aesthetic

The "pictures of the floating world" tradition of Edo-period Japan, made iconic by Hokusai, Hiroshige, and Utamaro. Flat areas of vibrant color are defined by precise carved lines, with compositions that balance decorative beauty and narrative content. Distinctive features include the absence of Western perspective, bold outlines, elegant patterning, and the characteristic texture of woodblock printing on washi paper.

## Background

- Color: Aged Washi (#F5F0E1)
- Texture: Visible woodblock print grain and paper fiber

## Typography

### Primary Font (Headlines)

Shippori Mincho or similar traditional Japanese serif. Regular to bold weight. Elegant brush-derived forms with cultural authenticity.

### Secondary Font (Body)

Noto Sans JP or similar clean sans-serif. Regular weight for readability while maintaining Japanese typographic harmony.

## Visual Elements

- Bold continuous outline key-block lines
- Flat color fills within outlined areas
- Absence of Western linear perspective
- Decorative textile patterns within figures
- Bokashi graduated color transitions (woodblock technique)
- Cartouche text boxes with calligraphy
- Natural subjects: waves, mountains, flora, fauna
- Compositional cropping influenced by Japanese aesthetics

## Style Rules

### Do

- Use bold outlines to define all forms
- Apply flat color areas within outlines
- Include traditional Japanese compositional principles
- Show woodblock printing texture and grain
- Maintain cultural authenticity in motifs and subjects

### Don't

- Apply Western perspective or realistic shading
- Use photographic or digital rendering
- Add modern graphic design elements
- Create smooth digital gradients
- Use colors outside traditional woodblock print range

## Best For

Japanese culture and history presentations, art history lectures, travel content, cultural exchange events, East Asian studies, museum exhibition materials
